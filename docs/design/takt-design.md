## Problem Statement

Every station on the line has a takt-time visual, but the Ops team cannot see who is doing what, who
needs coaching, or how to staff the line. The company is "data rich but information poor": every job
is already recorded as an MES transaction, yet leads "are not savvy enough to grab the data and make
meaning of it," and they have no access or report. The plant is currently overstaffed. The decision
about where to deploy people and who to coach is made by gut feel, and the sponsor wants it to be a
data-based discussion.

Takt turns existing transactions into two views: a line-health check for the ops manager and a
shift view for leads that replaces the hand-written sticky note. **It is a takt and coaching tool, not
a layoff tool.** The user corrected this framing explicitly during the session.

## Demand Evidence

- **Sponsor:** Brian, the ops manager. He wants supervisors and leads coaching operators on the line,
  and he wants "a dashboard that basically gives him some sort of health check of his line. That's
  gotta be takt, throughput, yield but all of them are tied together."
- **Urgency:** the plant is overstaffed and has no evidence base for deciding where to move people or
  who to coach.
- **Honest assessment:** demand is one internal sponsor, which is enough for an internal tool. The real
  adoption risk is the leads, not Brian. The sticky-note workflow is the only daily behavior already
  happening, so whether leads drop it is the real demand test (see Success Criteria).

## Status Quo

- Every day, each operator hands the lead a sticky note with the number of jobs they ran at their
  station. It is "slow and flawed" and can't be analyzed.
- There is no system for coaching. Leads coach whoever looks slow.
- Scrap is recorded where it is found (for example, post-fire sort), not where it is caused (for
  example, injection), and is not tied to an operator.
- Meanwhile, Cores Hub and Dies Hub (the MES) hold every job: operator ID, station, start and complete
  timestamps, and quantity. Leads can't reach this data.

## Target User and Narrowest Wedge

| | Brian (ops manager) | Supervisors | Leads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question | "Is my line healthy?" | "Which shift or station in my area needs attention?" | "Who on my shift needs help, with what?" |
| Scope | All areas | Their area(s), all shifts | Their area, their shift |
| Cadence | Daily glance | Daily | Every shift |

Brian's view wins the green light. The lead view is the wedge: it replaces the sticky note. All three
views share one data model and drill down line → station → operator.

## Constraints

- Read-only direct database access to Cores Hub and Dies Hub. Takt never writes to the MES.
- Zero new data entry for operators.
- Developed on the builder's laptop, then shipped as a Docker image to an IT-hosted VM like other plant
  tools. IT owns it after handoff, so the stack must be standard and documented, with no hand-run steps.
- Tech transactions are known to be unreliable ("the techs are bad at transactions").
- Scrap-to-cause attribution is probabilistic. For example, voids, negatives, and UVCs found at
  post-fire sort probably come from injection, but not always.

## Premises (agreed; 1 and 6 revised after the second opinion)

1. **(Revised)** The problem is "data rich, information poor." v1 adds no data entry and reads Cores Hub
   and Dies Hub read-only. **Neither counts nor durations are trusted by default. Each is trusted per
   station only after the audit:**
   - counts must reconcile against the sticky notes;
   - durations must pass the trust grade (premise 6).
2. Three views share one data model: Brian's Line Health, the supervisor's area view, and the lead's
   My Shift, which replaces the sticky note.
3. v1 measures yield at line and station level, counted where scrap is found, by scrap code. Tracing
   quality back to an operator (the fuzzy per-station scrap-code mapping) is v2.
4. An operator is only ever compared with others on the same station and part. **In v1, the context
   shown is what transactions and the roster can support:**
   - part mix;
   - transaction gaps;
   - an upstream output gap (the upstream station completed nothing during that window, from that
     station's own transactions);
   - tenure.

   True downtime and blocked time arrive with v2 equipment data. There is no plant-wide leaderboard.
   Operator comparisons are visible only to the roles in Component 7 and are never posted on the floor.
5. **Takt time = planned available time ÷ customer demand, in seconds per unit**, computed per part
   family from that family's demand at the line. Breaks and planned maintenance are subtracted from
   available time; unplanned downtime is not. One definition applies across Cores and Dies.
6. **(Revised)** v1 grades every **station-and-part** combination green, amber, or red for durations,
   using one rule set (Component 4). Separately, it flags each station **count-trusted** or not. The two
   combine through one display matrix (Component 4a), applied everywhere. Batch stations grade red by
   definition. Equipment data stays in v2 unless a **flow** station Brian cares about grades red; a batch
   station's red grade does not trigger it.
7. Success means leads stop collecting sticky notes and Brian opens the health check daily without
   being asked.

## Cross-Model Perspective (Claude subagent, cold read)

- **Steelman:** Takt compares the same station and part across operators, so a lead can tell whether
  a gap comes from the person, the part, or the process. That is attribution, not ranking. Staffing
  math (work content divided by takt gives the headcount needed per station) turns "we're overstaffed"
  into a decision about where to put people.
- **Most revealing answer:** the sticky note. It is the only daily workflow with a named user, so it is
  the wedge. "Build for the lead's 3pm sheet first and roll it up for Brian."
- **Challenged premise (Critical):** "Transactions do not miss anything" sits next to "techs are bad
  at transactions." Counts can be complete while timestamps are fiction: jobs batch-closed at shift end,
  jobs left open over breaks, and the kiln is a batch process. Every coaching number depends on
  timestamps nobody has validated. **The user accepted this, which produced the revised premises 1 and 6.**
- **Prototype suggestion:** Python, DuckDB, and Streamlit for one Cores area, with the audit first.
  The user chose the full app instead (below), keeping the audit as step 1.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Audit-first Streamlit sheet.** Ready to demo in days. Rejected: the user wants to build the
  final form that IT will own, rather than an MVP that gets rewritten.
- **B: Full plant app.** Chosen, with Django in place of FastAPI.
- **C: Push-first shift sheets (print, TV, email).** Not chosen as the product. The print sheet is in v1
  (Component 6). Brian's daily email is deferred to v1.1; trigger: Brian is not opening Line Health on 4

## Recommended Approach: B — Takt as a Django plant app

**Stack:** Django and Postgres, server-rendered Django templates with htmx for drill-down, Docker Compose (three containers: web, scheduler, Postgres), and an IT-hosted VM. App icon: a tuk-tuk. *(Eng review D1: smaller arrangement.)*

### 1. Ingest

A Django management command (`manage.py ingest`), run by cron (supercronic) in the scheduler container, pulls new
transactions from Cores Hub and Dies Hub read-only into raw staging tables.

- **Upsert key:** the MES transaction ID. Incremental selection uses the MES last-modified column if one
  exists (Open Question 1), otherwise the start timestamp.
- **Look-back:** every run re-reads the trailing 48 hours. A nightly run re-reads the trailing 7 days, so
  jobs closed late, batch-closed at shift end, or corrected after the fact overwrite their earlier copies.
- **MES load guard (eng review D9):** ingest runs every 5 minutes. Before production ingest, IT confirms
  either a read replica or reporting copy, or indexes on the selection column and the MES ID. Queries
  run in 5,000-row batches with a 30 s statement timeout. The nightly 7-day re-read runs at 02:00 plant
  time. Each run logs rows read and query time.
- **Long-open jobs (eng review D3):** every ingest run also re-reads all Jobs still open, by MES ID,
  regardless of age, so a job closed after the 7-day look-back still lands.
- **Deleted MES rows (eng review D4):** not detected in v1. Upgrade trigger: the MES is shown to
  hard-delete corrected rows, or a confirmed dispute traces to a deleted row.
- **Time normalization (eng review D2):** each hub has a configured plant timezone in the admin. Ingest
  converts MES timestamps to UTC (`USE_TZ = True`, `zoneinfo` for DST) and stamps every Job with
  `shift_id` and `production_date`. A shift belongs to the production date it **starts** on, so a
  23:00-07:00 shift is one day. `manage.py restamp_shifts` re-stamps affected Jobs when `ShiftCalendar`
  changes.
- **Open jobs** (no complete timestamp yet) are stored with `completed_at = NULL`. They are excluded from
  counts and durations until a later read closes them.

Both hubs map to **one shared schema** from day one:
- `Operator` (name, `hire_date`)
- `OperatorAlias` (hub, hub operator ID → Operator)
- `Station` (line, sequence position, type: flow or batch)
- `Area/Line`, `Part` (part family)
- `Routing` (part family, station, visit count). It is imported from the MES routing if one exists
  (Open Question 3); otherwise the builder, later the plant admin, maintains it in the admin.
- `Job` (MES transaction ID, source hub, operator alias, station, part, `started_at`, `completed_at`,
  quantity, `excluded` flag, exclusion reason)
- `ScrapEvent` (MES ID, source hub, timestamp, shift, part, station found, scrap code, quantity)
- `ShiftCalendar` (shift start and end, breaks, planned maintenance)
- `TaktConfig` (demand per part family per day)
- `Dispute`

### 2. Operator identity across hubs

`OperatorAlias` lives in the Django admin. The builder owns the mapping until handoff, then a plant admin
(named at handoff) owns it. When a job arrives with an unmapped alias:
- it **counts** toward station and line totals;
- it appears in My Shift as an "Unmapped operator (hub ID)" row;
- it is **excluded** from operator comparisons;
- it lands in an admin queue to be mapped.

Mapping it backfills its history.

### 3. Metric layer

SQL views or materialized views in Postgres, versioned in the repo. The UI only reads them.

- **Line pace (count-based, no durations):** good units completed at the line's final station ÷ units
  required so far. Units required = Σ over part families of (elapsed planned available time ÷ that
  family's takt time). Shown as a percentage; 100% means exactly on takt. Because it uses only
  final-station counts, line pace needs only that station's counts to be trusted, not any durations.
- **Station pace:** the station's completed good units ÷ the station's units required. A station's
  units required sums **only over the part families routed through that station**, multiplied by the
  number of visits a family makes to it. The routing comes from the station registry (Open Question 3).
- **Good units:** if `Job.quantity` is good output, it is used directly. If it is gross output, good
  units = quantity − scrap found at that station for the same part and shift. Which one it is depends
  on Open Question 1; the yield denominator uses good units + scrap either way.
- **Zero or missing demand (eng review D7):** a family with demand 0, or with no `TaktConfig` row for the
  day, adds 0 units required and is listed "no demand set". When units required is 0, pace shows "—"
  with the reason, never 0% or ∞. A missing row also raises a red warning in the admin.
- **Bottleneck:** the station with the lowest station pace among stations whose counts are trusted.
  Line Health shows coverage ("bottleneck chosen from 6 of 8 stations").
- **Operator rate (green combinations only):** units per hour on a station-and-part ÷ the median for that
  station and part over the trailing 4 weeks. Shown alongside the part's takt rate (3600 ÷ takt time)
  for reference.
- **Yield:**
  - **Station yield** = good units ÷ (good units + scrap units recorded at that station), per part.
  - **Line yield** = rolled throughput yield, the product of station yields along the line.
  - Scrap is attributed to the station where it was **found**, by scrap code.
- **Transaction gap:** the operator's shift time, minus breaks, minus time covered by their jobs. It is
  labeled "time with no transaction," not "idle."
- **Upstream output gap:** windows of 15 minutes or more in which the upstream station completed nothing.

### 4. Trust grade

One code path. The Assignment's audit is this job run once over history, so the audit and the nightly
job always agree. For each station-and-part, over the trailing 4 weeks, it computes three shares:

- **D:** durations under 10% or over 300% of the median;
- **C:** completes in the last 30 minutes of a shift, or sharing an identical timestamp with 3 or more
  other jobs;
- **O:** jobs that overlap another job by the same operator.

Grades:
- **Green:** D, C, and O are each under 10%.
- **Red:** any of them is over 25%, or the station is typed `batch`.
- **Amber:** everything else.

The thresholds live in the Django admin. The builder tunes them after the first audit, and the plant
admin owns them after handoff. A station's badge shows the grade covering the majority of its volume.

### 4a. Count trust and the display matrix

**Count trust** is a per-station flag, stored in the admin with who set it, when, and the evidence.

How it is established:
- **Pilot area:** the reconciliation in The Assignment.
- **Each later area, including Dies Hub stations:** before that area goes live, a one-week
  reconciliation runs, against the lead's tally where one still exists, or otherwise against a
  physical-count sample of 20 jobs per station (bin, traveler, or label counts).
- **New stations:** the same 20-job physical sample.

Pass rules:
- **Tally reconciliation:** the same 5% match and 10-job adjudication as The Assignment.
- **Physical sample:** passes when at least 19 of 20 jobs match the transaction quantity exactly, or the
  20-job total is within 5%.

Re-establishing trust after revocation: once the supervisor reports that transaction discipline is
fixed, a fresh 20-job physical sample is run. If it passes, the plant admin sets the flag again and
records the evidence.

Who sets it: the area supervisor proposes; the builder (later the plant admin) sets the flag.

How it is revoked:
- automatically, when confirmed count disputes exceed 5% of a station's jobs over a rolling 2 weeks;
- or manually, by the plant admin.

| | Duration grade green | Amber | Red (including batch) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Count-trusted** | Operator counts and rates; station pace | Operator counts, rates "n/a"; station pace | Operator counts only (no rates, comparisons, or transaction gap); station pace |
| **Not count-trusted** | "Counts unverified." No operator counts or rates; excluded from station pace and bottleneck | Same | Same |

- **Trust display (design review D16):** trust is shown with words and a grey outline badge, **never fill
  colour**: "Verified ✓" (green grade), "Counts only #" (amber), "Not graded yet …", "Excluded –"
  (red/batch), "Counts unverified ?" (not count-trusted). Colour is reserved for performance status, and
  even there it always comes with a status word.
- **Not graded yet (design review D11):** a station-part with under 4 weeks of history displays as amber,
  labeled "Not graded yet (needs 4 weeks; N days so far)".
- **Print sheet:** shows exactly the operator counts the matrix allows, and nothing else.
- **Line pace:** **suppressed** ("unavailable: final-station counts unverified") when the line's final
  station is not count-trusted. Line yield and the bottleneck (over trusted stations) still show.

### 5. Batch stations (the kiln)

Station type is set in the admin. The audit also suggests batch stations by detecting jobs that share
identical start and complete timestamps. For batch stations:
- there are no operator rates;
- station-level metrics are batches per shift, units per batch, batch cycle time (load to unload), and
  station pace from counts;
- operator display follows the "Red" column of the matrix in Component 4a.

### 6. Views

See the wireframe below.

- **Line Health (Brian):** one row per line with line pace, throughput vs demand, line yield with its
  top scrap code, the bottleneck with its coverage, and a 14-day trend. Below each row is a **rule-based
  summary sentence; no LLM is involved.**
  - Each of pace and yield is classified as normal, low, or high against its 14-day baseline
    (±1 standard deviation).
  - The classification picks one of nine templates, for example: "On pace, yield {n} pts under
    baseline, mostly {top code}. Look upstream at {cause station}, not at pace." The cause station comes
    from a static scrap-code hint in the admin; it is a hint, not attribution.
  - The bottleneck sentence is appended when the line is behind.
- **Line Health layout (design review D3):** a plain table, one row per line, with no bordered boxes.
  Each row **leads with a status word** (On pace / Behind / Yield problem / No demand set) and the
  summary sentence at 18px. Pace, throughput, yield, bottleneck and the 14-day sparkline sit below as
  14px tabular-number evidence.
- **Pace and throughput (design review D4):** one column, "104% · +40 units". The raw good/required
  counts appear in the station drill-down and as a tooltip.
- **Area (supervisor):** Line Health filtered to the supervisor's area, plus a shift-over-shift
  comparison.
- **Area layout (design review D7):** content runs top to bottom in this order:
  1. an open-disputes strip (count, overdue count, oldest age, "Review" link), shown only when disputes
     are open;
  2. the Line Health rows for the supervisor's lines;
  3. a 3-shift comparison table showing pace and yield per line.
- **My Shift (lead):**
  - grouped by station and part, never ranked across stations;
  - per operator: jobs, quantity, and (green only) rate vs the median;
  - time with no transaction, upstream output gap, and tenure under 90 days;
  - a per-row "this number looks wrong" dispute button.
- **"Worth a conversation" list (design review D5):** My Shift opens with up to 3 names per **green**
  station-part group, each with its context reason (for example "Sam · Injection 3 · −20% vs median ·
  tenure < 90 days"), above the grouped table. It is never plant-wide and never on the print sheet.
- **Row order (design review D6):** within each station-part group, rows are alphabetical by operator
  name. Rate columns are **not sortable**.
- **Dispute flow (design review D14):**
  - "This looks wrong" on an operator row opens that operator's job list for the shift inline.
  - The lead ticks one or more jobs and picks a reason: wrong quantity, wrong operator, job not mine,
    time looks wrong, or other with text.
  - A **"My disputes"** strip at the top of My Shift shows each dispute as Open / Confirmed / Rejected,
    with the supervisor's note.
- **Print sheet:** the sticky-note replacement. It lists each operator's job and quantity counts where
  the matrix in Component 4a allows them, plus station and part totals. It has **no rates, medians, or
  comparisons**, and the footer reads "For lead use."

- **Dispute queue (design review D8):**
  - a table sorted oldest first; overdue rows sit at the top with the text label "Overdue", not colour
    alone;
  - columns: age in shifts, station and part, job ID and time, operator, raised by, reason;
  - each row expands inline (htmx) to show the job's MES values with Confirm and Reject buttons;
  - **both Confirm and Reject require a note**, which is shown back to the lead.

- **Print sheet layout (D20):** a print stylesheet that hides the nav.
  - US Letter portrait, black and white.
  - Header: line, shift, date, "Data as of 2:38 PM" (latest ingest for the hub) and "Printed 2:41 PM",
    plus "late-closing jobs may change totals". If the hub is stale (30-minute rule), the header reads
    "STALE: data stopped at 6:40 AM" *(eng re-review D6)*.
  - One section per station-part, with page breaks only between stations.
  - Columns: operator, jobs, quantity, and a blank **Correction** column for handwritten fixes.
  - Trust is shown as the D16 words.
  - Footer: "For lead use. Not for posting." and page X of Y.
- **Default time windows (D21):**
  - Line Health opens on the **last completed production day** (all shifts).
  - Area opens on the **last completed shift**, with the current shift one click away.
  - My Shift opens on the lead's **current shift, live**.
  - Every screen has the same shift/date picker, and the header always names the window.
- **14-day sparkline (D22):**
  - Two thin lines: pace in ink, yield in `--text-2` grey.
  - Each line has a faint baseline ±1 SD band, and today's point is marked.
  - The accessible name is a text summary ("Pace 14-day trend: 98% to 104%; yield 82% to 71%").
- **Excluded jobs (D23):** the operator row shows an "N jobs excluded" link. In the job list, excluded jobs
  stay visible with strikethrough, the reason, who confirmed it, and when ("Excluded: wrong quantity,

  data exists, it shows "New data from 2:45 PM · Refresh"; the table swaps only when the lead clicks.
  There is one polite aria-live announcement per notice, and open dispute forms are never touched.
- **Wireframe corrections (restating the approved plan, no new decision):**
  - Line rows show no cycle time; line pace is count-only.
  - The bottleneck sentence appears only when a line is behind, with coverage text.
  - Kiln operator counts follow matrix 4a.
  - Operator detail is visible to the lead, the area supervisor and Brian.

### 7. Auth and roles

**Role-based access ships with the first operator-level screen, not at handoff.**
- In the pilot, Django local accounts are created only for named pilot users.
- SSO or LDAP via `django-auth-ldap` (matching IT's other plant tools) replaces local accounts before
  rollout beyond the pilot.

Roles:

| Role | Scope |
|---|---|
| ops manager | All areas |
| supervisor | Their areas, all shifts |
| lead | Their area and shift |
| admin | Configuration only, no operator detail |

**Operator identity policy (eng re-review D2):** every view, htmx partial, print view and the dispute
queue renders operator identity only through `operator_display(user, operator, context)`. It returns
named, letter or hidden from the user's role scope and the **Gate 1 admin setting**. A sweep test
requests every operator-bearing URL as each role, before and after Gate 1. Templates never render
`operator.name` directly.

**Gate 1 letters (eng re-review D3):** each full page load creates a random page token. Letters order
operators by HMAC(token, operator_id). Every htmx partial on the page sends the token (`hx-vals`), so
letters match within the page; a new load gets a new token.

### 8. Disputes

A lead's dispute creates **one `Dispute` row per job** (job, reason, who raised it, `submission_id`). A
multi-job submission creates N rows sharing a `submission_id` and reason; the supervisor decides per
job, with "apply to all in this submission" *(eng re-review D4)*. It notifies the area's supervisor
through an open-dispute count badge in their Area view header and nav plus a dispute queue page;
disputes older than 2 shifts show red. No email *(eng review D6)*. The supervisor decides within 2 shifts:
- **Confirmed:** the job gets `excluded = true` with a reason. It drops out of rates, and out of counts
  too if the count itself is wrong. The supervisor fixes the MES record. Takt keeps the exclusion even if
  the MES record later changes, until the supervisor clears it.
- **Rejected:** the dispute is closed with a note visible to the lead.
